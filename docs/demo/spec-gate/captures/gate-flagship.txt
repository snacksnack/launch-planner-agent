## Findings
- **blocker** `missing_nfr` (Sequencing and the freeze)
  > "Cut over to Cloud in production, hypercare, then decommission on-prem."
  This is a migration spec whose entire subject is a one-way cutover with a hard license deadline forcing on-prem decommission shortly after go-live. There is no stated rollback or abort plan if the production cutover fails or surfaces critical issues during hypercare — for a migration of this scale (450 users, 20 projects, client data), that absence is conspicuous and would leave the team with no defined fallback.
  Suggested rewrite: Add a rollback/abort criteria section: define the conditions under which cutover is aborted or rolled back, how long on-prem is kept warm as a fallback, and who has authority to trigger rollback.
- **warning** `missing_nfr` (Why now / what success looks like)
  > "On-prem instance shut down and decommissioned before the license expires."
  The doc sets 'no data loss' as a success criterion but the plan is to shut down and decommission on-prem shortly after go-live with no stated retention of a backup or archive to fall back on if reconciliation problems are discovered after decommission.
  Suggested rewrite: Specify a retention window: keep a verified, restorable backup/export of the on-prem instance for N days/weeks after decommission, in case reconciliation issues surface post-cutover.
- **warning** `untestable_criterion` (Why now / what success looks like)
  > "All 450 users migrated with the right permissions and single sign-on working."
  'The right permissions' has no observable pass/fail definition — two reviewers could disagree on whether a given user's Cloud permissions are 'right' without a defined mapping or acceptance check.
  Suggested rewrite: Define 'right permissions' concretely, e.g. 'each user's Cloud role/group membership matches a signed-off permission mapping produced during discovery, verified via an automated diff.'
- **warning** `untestable_criterion` (Why now / what success looks like)
  > "No data loss: ticket counts, comments, and attachments reconcile against on-prem."
  'Reconcile against on-prem' does not specify the method or acceptable tolerance for reconciliation, and the open questions section confirms this is still unresolved ('formal sign-off vs spot check'), meaning the stated success criterion cannot yet be judged pass/fail.
  Suggested rewrite: Define the reconciliation method and tolerance explicitly, e.g. 'ticket counts, comment counts, and attachment counts must match on-prem exactly per project, verified via automated report, signed off by each project owner.'
- **warning** `conflicting_requirement` (Scope & known problems)
  > "Before we can plan waves we need to inventory all 20 projects, their workflows, and the custom fields"
  The spec states wave planning cannot happen until the full inventory of all 20 projects/workflows/custom fields is complete ('Before we can plan waves we need to inventory...'), yet the 'Sequencing and the freeze' and 'Rough milestones' sections already lock in a four-wave plan and specific dates (pilot 2026-11-10, bulk end of Feb 2027) without that discovery having been done.
  Suggested rewrite: Either mark the wave sequence and milestone dates as provisional pending discovery output, or clarify that discovery is scoped to run in parallel with a coarse wave plan that will be refined once inventory completes.
- **warning** `unowned_scope` (Scope & known problems)
  > "The QA lead owns that sign-off."
  Unlike the Legal and Finance approvals, which are pinned to named individuals (Helen, Grace), the plugin audit sign-off — a hard prerequisite for migrating any real user — is owned only by a role ('the QA lead'), not a specific accountable person.
  Suggested rewrite: Name the specific individual who owns the plugin compatibility audit sign-off (e.g., 'Jordan, QA Lead') the same way Legal (Helen) and Finance approval (Grace) are named.
- **warning** `unowned_scope` (The parts Legal and Security care about)
  > "Security and SRE need to review the Cloud configuration — SSO, IP allow-listing, audit logging — and there will be no production cutover without a security and SRE sign-off."
  The security/SRE sign-off is described as a hard gate on production cutover, but is attributed to teams ('Security and SRE') rather than a named accountable person, unlike other gating approvals in the doc that are pinned to individuals.
  Suggested rewrite: Name a specific individual accountable for the security/SRE cutover sign-off, e.g. 'Alex (SRE) and Sam (Security) jointly own this gate.'
- **warning** `unstated_assumption` (Scope & known problems)
  > "a plugin compatibility audit sign-off is a prerequisite for migrating users"
  The plan schedules a pilot migration for ~2026-11-10 while also stating that no real user may be migrated until the plugin audit is signed off, but no target date is given for that audit — the schedule implicitly assumes it will finish in time without saying so.
  Suggested rewrite: State a target completion date for the plugin compatibility audit and confirm it lands before the pilot milestone (~2026-11-10); if not, adjust the pilot date.
- **nit** `missing_nfr` (Scope & known problems)
  > "contractor spend has to be approved by Finance before anyone gets onboarded"
  The spec flags that contractor spend needs Finance approval but never states a cost ceiling or budget envelope, which is relevant given the project explicitly frames people/budget as a constraint.
  Suggested rewrite: State the approved budget ceiling for contractor spend (e.g., 'up to $X for two contractors through Q1 2027') rather than only 'requires Finance approval'.
- **nit** `ambiguous_quantifier` (Background)
  > "with as little disruption to the 15 teams as we can manage"
  'As little disruption as we can manage' carries no measurable threshold — there's no way to later judge whether the migration met this bar.
  Suggested rewrite: Replace with a measurable disruption budget, e.g. 'no more than N hours of read-only downtime per team during cutover, communicated 2 weeks in advance.'
